## The principle

We translate for the simple understanding of the text, oriented toward what the
author of the book intended when they wrote it.

## What this means in practice

- Where a word or phrase can be read several ways, we choose the reading the
  author most plausibly meant, not the most homiletically interesting one.
- A translation that is defensible under this principle is **not an error**,
  even when a reader prefers a different reading. It is a disagreement with the
  methodology, and the right response is to explain the choice.
- A translation that misreads the grammar, drops or invents content, or renders
  a term in a way the author could not have meant **is an error**.
